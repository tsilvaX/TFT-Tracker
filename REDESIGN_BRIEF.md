# TFT Tracker Redesign Brief

## Visual Personality

**The Matchroom Ledger** is a post-game review desk: part tournament broadcast, part analyst notebook, part polished desktop application. It should feel authored by a serious TFT player, not assembled from dashboard components.

## Typography

Use Outfit as the primary interface and data face. Hierarchy comes from scale, weight, alignment, and whitespace. Numerals are tabular and authoritative; labels stay compact, sentence case, and calm.

## Composition

Use an asymmetric player masthead and a 12-column analytical canvas. Important numbers may sit in open space instead of inside small cards. Full-width rules and tonal bands separate workflows. Density varies by content: the match ledger is compact, the trend plot is expansive, and tables are disciplined.

## Color

The foundation is ink blue-black and mineral off-white. Violet belongs to identity and selection. Cobalt-to-red placement colors carry game meaning. Amber is reserved for honest notes and warnings. Use flat color and tonal depth; avoid glow, glossy gradients, and decorative halos.

## Data Visualization

Placement history behaves like a tactile match ledger. Trend data lives on a ruled plotting field with a precise hover cursor. Placement breakdowns read as editorial rows. Tables emphasize rank, sample size, placement, and top-four rate through alignment and contrast rather than badges.

## Motion And Interaction

Use 140-220ms interruptible transitions. Buttons press, the tab rule slides, chart focus follows the pointer, filter focus is unambiguous, and data refreshes retain context. Tab content changes as a shallow layered transition. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and avoid ambient movement.

## TFT Motifs

Use clipped corners sparingly, reserve hex geometry for trait tiers, echo the board edge with thin rules, integrate the tactician into player identity, and preserve the eight-placement color language.

## Eliminate

Remove the oversized logo card, nested cards, repetitive pills, purple gradient atmosphere, shiny borders, tag clouds, equal-weight stat boxes, generic rounded containers, and large empty dashboard regions.
